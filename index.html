The insight

AI shouldn't always answer the question. It should first determine whether the evidence is sufficient.

Most AI systems optimize for "what happened?" Statum asks: "does the available evidence justify that conclusion?"

SUPPORTED

Evidence sufficiently converges across sensors and sources.

CONFLICTED

Material evidence disagrees — resolution requires more than a majority vote.

UNDERDETERMINED

Evidence is insufficient to support any conclusion yet.

The evidence base

Three sensors that disagree for real, physical reasons

Statum's reconciliation logic exists because these sensors have genuinely different, well-documented blind spots — not because AI needs something to reason about.

Sentinel-2 — Optical

10m resolution · 13 spectral bands · ~5-day revisit

Visible and near-infrared imagery used to detect vegetation change, canopy structure, and land-cover class. The default evidence source — and the first to fail when cloud cover moves in.

Sentinel-1 — SAR

C-band radar · 10m resolution · day/night, all-weather

Synthetic aperture radar sees through cloud cover and darkness by measuring reflected radar pulses. Often the only usable evidence during the rainy seasons when most disputed change actually happens.

VIIRS — Nighttime lights

500m resolution · daily global revisit · Suomi NPP / NOAA-20

Detects fire, gas flaring, and human activity via light emission at night. Structurally blind in non-electrified areas — a known failure mode Statum is built to flag, not silently trust.

Contextual data

Admin boundaries · historical baselines · precipitation records

Reduces false conflicts caused by known seasonal patterns, administrative reclassification, or prior investigation outcomes for the same location.

The product

An AI investigator for physical-world claims

Input: a claim, a location, and a time window. Statum runs it through four stages.

01

Assemble

Retrieve relevant physical-world evidence — Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, VIIRS, and contextual data.

02

Reconcile

Compare evidence across space, time, sensors, and quality.

03

Resolve

Produce a verdict: SUPPORTED, CONFLICTED, or UNDERDETERMINED.

04

Escalate

Recommend the next-best evidence to resolve what remains uncertain.

Worked example
Claim: Land-use change occurred between 2022–2025.
CONFLICTED — SAR and optical observations support physical change, while nighttime-light evidence does not provide corresponding support.
Next: Acquire additional SAR evidence from the missing period.
Technical differentiation

Statum is not another satellite classification model

Existing geospatial AI
Image → Model → Prediction

A single-pass classifier. No reasoning about whether the evidence justifies the output.

Statum
Claim → Evidence retrieval → Cross-modal reconciliation → Conflict detection → Sufficiency → Decision / Abstention → Next-best evidence

The defensible layer is the evidence-resolution protocol and evidence graph — not the underlying imagery. Every investigation strengthens the system's judgment about which evidence resolves which kind of uncertainty.

Why Google

Future of Knowledge: reasoning over incomplete evidence

Statum sits directly under Google's Future of Knowledge theme — building AI systems that help institutions extract reliable knowledge from incomplete and fragmented data.

Gemini

REASONING

Multimodal reasoning (Gemini 3 family) ingests optical imagery, SAR backscatter, and structured metadata together — evaluating whether evidence is sufficient, not just what one image shows. Multi-step, agentic tool use drives evidence retrieval across sensors.

Gemma

TRIAGE

Open-weight Gemma models handle lightweight, specialized triage — cheap enough to run per-tile across a large screening backlog, reserving Gemini's deeper reasoning for cases that actually need it.

Earth Engine

DATA

Google Earth Engine's petabyte-scale public catalog — including the full Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, Landsat, and VIIRS archives — gives Statum planetary-scale access and compute without operating any satellite ground infrastructure of its own.
